The Schwinger model provides a useful theoretical framework for studying nonperturbative phenomena relevant to quantum chromodynamics (QCD), including
chiral symmetry breaking and topological effects. In this work, we investigate the temperature and vacuum-angle (θ) dependence of the chiral condensate in the
bosonized lattice Schwinger model using the Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) algorithm. The chiral condensate ⟨ψψ⟩ is evaluated for the massless and massive models at finite temperature and for nonzero values of θ. The results show
that the chiral condensate decreases with increasing temperature and approaches the behavior expected for restored chiral symmetry at sufficiently high temperatures. The dependence on the vacuum angle exhibits a nontrivial variation of the
condensate, with the observed behavior being consistent with the possibility of spontaneous CP symmetry breaking in the appropriate parameter regime. The
HMC simulations also show increased autocorrelation and statistical uncertainty as the lattice spacing is reduced, indicating a slowing down of the sampling process. The inclusion of topological effects further increases autocorrelation times,
indicating topological slowing down in the simulations. Overall, the simulation reproduces the qualitative behavior reported in previous studies and demonstrates the effectiveness of HMC for investigating thermodynamic and topological
properties of the bosonized lattice Schwinger model.
